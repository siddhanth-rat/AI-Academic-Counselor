Depending on why you’re applying for a study visa in Germany, you can apply for one of the following visa types: Germanyvisa service

Full-time academic studies
This type of study visa is issued to foreign students following a study program such as a Bachelor’s, Master’s, or Post Doctoral Degree.

German language courses for studies
You can apply for this type of study visa for the sole purpose of entering Germany to learn the language and then enroll in a full-time study program.

State preparatory college ‘Studienkolleg’
A course you’ll have to undertake as a foreigner whose school-leaving certificate isn’t recognized in Germany. Once completing the course, you’ll be sitting the test “Feststellungsprüfung” and receive a certificate of completion. Then, you’ll be able to apply for university admission in Germany.

Propaedeutic course
A visa to participate in a preparatory course to help you prepare for further academic studies in Germany, particularly in specialized fields.

Mandatory preliminary internship
This visa is required if your degree has a mandatory internship requirement before you start your academic program.

Please  note that if your study program lasts less than 90 days, for example, an exchange study program, you can apply for a Schengen visa instead. This visa is short-term and will be issued for the duration of your program.


The language skills required to commence studying may vary depending on the degree programme and in specific cases. Your language level must generally be sufficient for you to complete the study programme in the respective language of instruction. To attend a preparatory college or comparable educational institutions, proof of sufficient German language skills must also be provided. The language skills can be confirmed by the university, the preparatory college or a comparable educational institution as well as by a recognised language certificate.

In order to study or to search for a university place, you must be able to prove that you can support yourself for the duration of your stay in Germany. To commence studying, you must prove that you have at your disposal at least 992 euros per month and to look for a university place at least 1,091 euros per month. You can provide proof of this, for instance, with a blocked account, a Declaration of Commitment or a scholarship.

If you have to pay tuition fees in addition to the semester fees, you must also provide suitable proof that you have sufficient financial resources. This also applies to fees for a preparatory college or a preparatory language course.

Your livelihood can also be additionally secured through secondary employment. With a residence permit for study purposes, you can work in Germany for up to 140 days a year. This is not valid for part-time jobs for students. Self-employed activities are not permitted.



## Applying for a German Student Visa Online
To apply for a student visa online, you have to set up an account with the Consular Services Portal. Select your country of residence from the dropdown menu, and you will be redirected to the application portal. Once there, you have to:

Fill out the visa application form. You’ll need to answer questions related to your personal information, previous education as well as your upcoming studies.
Attach the required documents. You’ll need to attach electronic copies of the required documents, such as your passport and the letter of acceptance. The documents have to be in PDF, JPG or PNG format and no larger than 10 MB.’
Once you finish the online application, you still need to schedule an appointment with the German embassy or consulate, where you will submit your fingerprints. At the end of the online application, you will receive a confirmation code, which you must have with you when you appear at the embassy.

## Applying for a German Student Visa In-Person
To apply in-person at a German embassy or consulate, you have to:

Schedule a visa appointment. You have to set up an appointment with the corresponding German embassy, consulate, or visa application center in your country.
Fill out the visa application form. You can fill out the application form through the VIDEX portal or by downloading the PDF version. At the end, you have to print a copy of the filled-out application and take it with you to the embassy/consulate.
Prepare the required documents. Along with your application form, you must also prepare several documents such as proof of financial support, your passport, proof of enrollment to university, and so on. You can find a detailed list of required documents below. Please make sure to translate and certify any documents that are not in English or German, or an equivalent acceptable language by the embassy or consulate.
Submit your application. On the day of your visa appointment, you must submit your application along with the rest of the other required documents. You may also be asked to sit down for a brief interview and answer questions regarding your study plans in Germany.
## After You Get Your Visa
After you receive your visa, you can travel to Germany. Within two weeks, you have to register your new address in the country and receive a certificate as proof of registration. Then, you must visit a local Foreigner’s Office “Ausländerbehörde”, and apply for your residence permit.

Please note that your initial student visa allows you to only enter the country, your residence permit is the one that allows you to stay and study in the country

## Tuition Fees in Germany
German public universities are largely tuition-free for both domestic and international students at the Bachelor's level and many Master's programs. However, there are important exceptions:
- Semester Fee (Semesterbeitrag): All universities charge a mandatory semester fee of approximately €150–€350 per semester. This is NOT tuition — it covers student union membership, administration, and usually includes a free public transport (Semester-Ticket) valid across the entire local public transport network.
- Baden-Württemberg Exception: Since 2017, the state of Baden-Württemberg charges non-EU international students a tuition fee of €1,500 per semester (€3,000 per year). This applies to universities in cities such as Stuttgart, Freiburg, Heidelberg, and Karlsruhe.
- Private Universities: Private universities in Germany (e.g., WHU, EBS, Jacobs University) charge significant tuition fees ranging from €10,000 to €30,000+ per year.
- Always check whether your specific university and state charge tuition fees before finalising your financial planning.

## BAföG Ineligibility for International Students
BAföG (Bundesausbildungsförderungsgesetz) is the German government’s financial support scheme for students, providing grants and interest-free loans. International students are generally NOT eligible for BAföG. Exceptions apply only to:
- EU/EEA citizens who have been working in Germany for a certain period.
- Non-EU students who have been granted specific long-term residence statuses (e.g., refugees, persons with humanitarian protection).
- Students whose parents have been living and working in Germany for several years.
For the vast majority of international students on a student visa, BAföG is not available. Funding must come from personal/family funds, the blocked account, scholarships, or part-time work.

## Blocked Account Refund Process (In Case of Visa Rejection or Withdrawal)
If your visa application is rejected by the German embassy/consulate, or if you decide to withdraw your visa application or university enrollment, you can get a full refund of your blocked account funds (€11,904).
- The official refund process:
  1. Obtain the official rejection letter (*Ablehnungsbescheid*) from the German embassy/consulate. If you withdrew your application, obtain a confirmation of application withdrawal or a university certificate of de-registration (*Exmatrikulationsbescheinigung*).
  2. Log in to your blocked account provider's portal (Expatrio, Fintiba, Coracle, etc.) and initiate the closing request.
  3. Upload the official documents as proof.
  4. Provide your home country bank account details (the IBAN/BIC/Swift code of the account you originally used to transfer the money, or your personal account).
  5. The provider will verify the documents with the German Federal Foreign Office. This verification and return transfer process typically takes 2 to 4 weeks.
- Fees: Most providers will refund the entire €11,904, but they may keep the initial setup fee (usually around €49–€89) to cover administrative costs. Any bank transfer fees for the international wire return are also deducted from your final payout.

How to Apply for a Student Residence Permit in Germany?
You apply for a student residence permit at the Ausländerbehörde (Foreigner’s Authority) after arriving in Germany. Beforehand, you usually have to get a student visa from a German  embassy or consulate in your home country. (Citizens of certain countries, such as the United States, Australia, Japan, and Canada, are exempt from the entry visa.)

Here’s a step-by-step description of how to get a student residence permit in Germany:

Step 1. Find a Local Ausländerbehörde
As soon as you get to Germany, you have to locate the Ausländerbehörde (you will see it referred to as the Foreigner’s Authority or Immigration Office). This is where you have to submit the application for a residence permit. It is basically where you head to for most procedures regarding your residence as an international living in Germany.

Visit their website or contact them to learn whether you need an appointment for your residence permit application. They may also have the required documents and forms listed.

Step 2. Register Your Address
Your next step is to register your address at the residents’ registration office (Bürgeramt or Bürgerbüro in German). You must register your address within 14 days of entering Germany and get a Registration Certificate (Meldebestätigung). You need the Meldebestätigung not only to apply for the residence permit but to open a bank account as well.

Opening a German Bank Account:
A German bank account is essential for receiving wages, paying rent, and managing day-to-day finances. You generally need the Meldebestätigung (registration certificate) to open a traditional bank account.
- N26 (Recommended for New Arrivals): N26 is a fully digital bank that is extremely popular with international students. It can be opened online with just your passport and can sometimes be set up even before you receive your Meldebestätigung, making it ideal for the first weeks in Germany.
- Traditional Banks: Deutsche Bank, Commerzbank, Sparkasse, and Postbank all offer student accounts, but typically require your Meldebestätigung, passport, and enrollment confirmation.
- Blocked Account Providers: Expatrio and Fintiba also offer accompanying bank accounts alongside the blocked account, which makes transitioning finances easier after arrival.

You can get the Meldebestätigung within a day. The documents required for the appointment at the Bürgeramt are:

The Proof of Residence Certificate (Wohnunggeberbestätigung). Your landlord should provide you with such a certificate when you move in. Alternatively, you could print out the form from the website of the Bürgeramt and ask them to fill it out.
The Registration Form. You will complete this at the Bürgeramt.
Your passport.
Your visa (if you needed one to enter).
Step 3. Obtain German Student Health Insurance
There are two health insurance options for international students in Germany:

- Public (statutory) health insurance (GKV): If you are under the age of 30 AND have not completed your 14th subject semester, you qualify for the subsidized student public health insurance. This costs approximately €120–€130 per month (which includes statutory health and long-term care insurance contributions) and offers comprehensive medical coverage with major providers like TK (Techniker Krankenkasse), AOK, or Barmer.
- Private health insurance (PKV): If you are 30 years of age or older, OR if you have finished your 14th subject semester, you are no longer eligible for the subsidized public student health scheme. You must either pay the much higher voluntary public insurance rate (often €200–€250+ per month) or purchase a private student insurance policy (e.g. from Mawista or DR-WALTER), which can range from €40 to €150+ per month depending on coverage.
Once you have health insurance coverage, you must obtain the health insurance certificate to submit along with your application. You must also obtain a waiver from public health insurance (Befreiung von der gesetzlichen Krankenversicherungpflicht) if you choose private health insurance.

Step 4. Prepare the Required Documents for a German Student Residence Permit
The documents required for a German student residence permit application at the Ausländerbehörde include:

Residence Permit Application Form. You can download this from the website of the Ausländerbehörde or obtain it in person at the office.
Your passport.
Your student visa (if applicable).
One passport-size picture.
Proof of financial means. Depending on your situation, this could be:
Proof of blocked account.
Proof of a scholarship.
Proof of financial support from your parents or a sponsor.
Health insurance certificate.
Certificate of enrollment or letter of approval from your university (if applicable).
Proof of enrollment in a German preparatory language school (if applicable).
Proof of conditional acceptance into a German university (if applicable).
Residence Registration Certificate.
Step 5. Submit Your Documents to the Ausländerbehörde
Once you have all the required documents, on the date of your appointment, you must submit them to the Ausländerbehörde to apply for your student residence permit. You will also enter an interview with an immigration officer.

Make sure you have enough money with you to pay for your residence permit. Some immigration offices accept credit or debit cards, but not all.

Once you submit your application, it takes around 5-6 weeks to receive your student residence permit.

## Working Rights With a Student Residence Permit in Germany
As of March 2024, as an international student in Germany, you can work part-time (20 hours per week) during the semester and full-time (40 hours per week) during breaks. This means that during a year, you can work a maximum of 140 full days or 280 half-days without requesting approval from the German Employment Agency. StudyAbroad

However, there are exceptions to the 140-day rule for certain working categories, such as student assistants or mandatory internships.


Here are your working options if you have a student residence permit in Germany:

Part-Time Job
You can take a part-time job in Germany. Your job should ideally be related to your course of study, but this is not mandatory. You can also work at local restaurants or cafes, as a babysitter, at a shop, etc.

You can only work 20 hours per week or 140 full days per year.

Internship
If you have to complete an internship as part of your study course, then the hours you work during the internship do not count towards the 140-day rule. If you are applying for an internship at your own will, then you are allowed to work only part-time.

Student Assistant
The 140-day rule does not apply if you find work as a student assistant at your university. But remember that you still have to inform the Ausländerbehörde that you have been offered a student assistant job and you want to work more hours.

Freelancing and Self-Employment
As an international student, you are not allowed to do freelance work or take up self-employment without requesting approval from the German Immigration Authority and Employment Authority.

If you meet the requirements, then you can request to switch to a German freelancer visa before finishing your studies. The Ausländerbehörde and Employment Agency will decide whether to grant you your request.

Naturally, if your request is approved, you can work as many hours as you wish, without restriction.

## Minijob — The €520/Month Tax-Free Work Option
One of the most popular and practical work arrangements for international students in Germany is the Minijob. A Minijob is a part-time job where you earn a maximum of €520 per month. Key benefits:
- No income tax on Minijob earnings.
- No social security contributions for the employee (the employer pays a flat-rate contribution).
- The hours worked in a Minijob do NOT count toward the 140-day annual work limit in the same way regular employment does.
- You can hold a Minijob even during the semester without it affecting your studies legally.
- You still need a Steuer-ID (Tax ID) to register a Minijob with an employer.

## German Tax ID (Steuer-Identifikationsnummer)
Every person who registers their address in Germany (Anmeldung) automatically receives a Steueridentifikationsnummer (Steuer-ID or Tax ID) sent by post to their registered address within 2–3 weeks of registration. This is a permanent 11-digit number.
- Your employer MUST have your Steuer-ID before they can process your payroll correctly. Without it, your employer is required by law to withhold tax at the maximum rate (Steuerklasse VI).
- Once you receive your Steuer-ID and provide it to your employer, any overpaid tax is typically corrected in the next payroll cycle or through an annual tax return.
- Your Steuer-ID stays with you for life in Germany. If you lose it, you can request a new letter from the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office).
- Income Tax Basics: Germany's tax-free personal allowance (Grundfreibetrag) is €11,784 per year (2024). If your annual earnings are below this, you generally pay no income tax. For earnings above this threshold, rates start at 14% and increase progressively.

Annual Tax Return (Steuererklärung):
As a working student, you are highly encouraged to file an annual tax return (Steuererklärung) with the tax office (Finanzamt).
- Why file: If you only worked part of the year or had multiple jobs, your employer might have deducted too much tax on the assumption that you earn that income all year. Filing a tax return allows you to get a refund of any overpaid tax.
- Deductions: You can deduct student-related expenses, such as semester fees, books, laptops used for study, study trips, and travel to university.
- Deadline: The standard deadline to submit a tax return yourself is 31 July of the following year (e.g. 31 July 2025 for the 2024 tax year).
- Tools: You can use the government's free portal (ELSTER) or student-friendly apps (like Taxfix, SteuerGo, or Wundertax) to make filing easier.

## After Graduation
After graduating, you have two options if you want to keep living in Germany: Germanytravel insurance

If you don’t have a job offer, you can switch to a job-seeker visa.
If you have a job offer, you can switch to a residence permit for work.
Switching to a Job-Seeker Visa
After you graduate, but before your student residence permit expires, you can switch to a residence permit as a job seeker. This permit lets you live in Germany for another 18 months post-graduation to look for a job.

Switching to a Residence Permit for Work
If you have a job offer as soon as you graduate, then you can just apply to change your immigration status to a residence permit for employment.

The residence permit for employment is issued for two years, and can continue to be extended as long as you still have a job.

If you meet the requirements set by the immigration authorities, then you can also apply for a residence permit and work as a freelancer in Germany.

## Travelling Within the Schengen Area
This is one of the most significant and often overlooked benefits of holding a German student residence permit. Once you have a valid German student residence permit (not just the initial entry visa), you are permitted to travel freely within the entire Schengen Area of 27 countries without needing a separate visa for each country.

The Schengen Area includes: Austria, Belgium, Croatia, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Italy, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden, and Switzerland.

Important rules:
- You must carry your valid passport and your German residence permit card whenever you travel within the Schengen Area.
- Your residence permit must be valid for the duration of your travel.
- You can stay in any other Schengen country for up to 90 days in any 180-day period as a visitor (you cannot work in another Schengen country on your German student permit).
- Travelling outside the Schengen Area (e.g., to the UK, USA, or your home country) requires any relevant visas for those countries and does not affect your German permit as long as you return before it expires.

## Driving in Germany on a Foreign License
If you want to drive in Germany, you must follow the strict time limit regarding foreign licenses:
- 6-Month Rule: International students from non-EU/EEA countries can legally drive in Germany on their valid home country driving license for up to 6 months after registering their address (Anmeldung). An official translation of your license (e.g., from ADAC or a sworn translator) or an International Driving Permit (IDP) is required alongside your original license.
- After 6 Months: Your foreign license becomes invalid for driving in Germany. To continue driving, you must obtain a German driving license (Führerschein).
  - EU/EEA license holders: Can drive indefinitely without exchanging, or exchange for a German license without a test.
  - Reciprocal countries (e.g., USA states, Canada provinces, Australia): Can exchange their license directly or by taking only the theory or practical test (depending on the country/state agreement).
  - Non-reciprocal countries (e.g., India, China, Turkey): You must register with a driving school (Fahrschule), pass the full German theory exam, and pass the practical driving test. This is an expensive and time-consuming process that can cost €1,500–€3,000+.
- Exception: In rare circumstances, you can apply at the local road licensing office (Fahrerlaubnisbehörde) to extend the 6-month period by another 6 months if you can prove you will not be staying in Germany for longer than 12 months in total.

## Vocational Training Visa (Ausbildung) in Germany
For students looking for an alternative to university study, Germany offers a vocational training pathway called *Ausbildung* (dual vocational training). This is a highly popular option that combines classroom study with hands-on work experience at a German company.

Key requirements for the Ausbildung Visa:
- Training Contract: You must have a signed vocational training contract with a German company/employer.
- Language Proficiency: You must have a minimum German language level of B1 (certified by Goethe-Institut, ÖSD, Telc, or TestDaF). Some high-skill programs (e.g., nursing or hospitality) require B2 German. English-taught Ausbildung programs are virtually non-existent.
- Academic Qualification: You must have a school-leaving certificate equivalent to a German *Hauptschulabschluss* or *Realschulabschluss* (generally equivalent to completing 10th or 12th grade in your home country).
- Federal Employment Agency Approval: The local Federal Employment Agency (*Agentur für Arbeit*) must approve the training contract, ensuring there are no qualifying German or EU applicants available for the training slot (priority check).
- Monthly Salary and Blocked Account Exemption: Unlike university students, Ausbildung participants receive a monthly salary (*Ausbildungsvergütung*) paid by their employer. If this salary is at least €927 net per month (as of 2024), you are completely exempt from the blocked account requirement. If your salary is below this threshold, you must cover the difference using a blocked account or a sponsorship letter.
- After Graduation: After completing your Ausbildung, you can stay in Germany on a job-seeker visa for up to 12 months to find a full-time job in your training field, or switch directly to a work visa if you have a job offer. Time spent on a vocational training visa also counts toward the residency requirements for permanent settlement.
