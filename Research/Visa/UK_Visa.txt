UK Visa

Overview
You can apply for a Student visa to study in the UK if you’re 16 or over and you:

have been offered a place on a course by a licensed student sponsor
have enough money to support yourself and pay for your course - the amount will vary depending on your circumstances
can speak, read, write and understand English
have consent from your parents if you’re 16 or 17 - you’ll need evidence of this when you apply
If you’re 16 or 17 and you want to study at an independent school in the UK, you may be eligible for a Child Student visa instead.

This visa has replaced the Tier 4 (General) student visa.

When to apply
When you can apply depends on whether you’re applying from inside or outside the UK.

Applying from outside the UK
The earliest you can apply for a visa is 6 months before you start your course.

Applying from inside the UK
The earliest you can apply is 3 months before your course starts.

You must apply before your current visa expires. Your new course must begin within 28 days of your current visa expiring.

How long it takes to get a decision
You’ll usually get a decision on your visa within:

3 weeks, if you’re outside the UK
8 weeks, if you’re inside the UK
You may be able to pay to get a faster decision. UKVI offers two expedited processing services (availability depends on the country and visa application centre):
- Priority Service: Costs an additional £500 on top of the standard application fee. You will typically receive a decision within 5 working days of your biometric appointment.
- Super Priority Service: Costs an additional £1,000 on top of the standard application fee. You will typically receive a decision by the end of the next working day after your biometric appointment.
Always check the availability of these services at your local visa application centre before finalizing your application timeline.

How long you can stay
How long you can stay depends on the length of your course and what study you’ve already completed in the UK.

If you’re 18 or over and your course is at degree level, you can usually stay in the UK for up to 5 years. If it’s below degree level, you can usually stay in the UK for up to 2 years.

Read the guidance to find out exactly how long you can stay.

Staying longer in the UK
You may be able to:

extend your visa if you’re eligible, for example to continue your studies in the UK
switch to a Student visa from another visa if you’re already in the UK
switch to a Graduate visa

Important Rule on Switching to Work Visas:
As of July 2023, international students are NOT allowed to switch from a Student visa to a work route (such as the Skilled Worker visa) before they have fully completed their course.
A Graduate visa will allow you  to stay in the UK after successfully completing your course for:

2 years if you apply on or before 31 December 2026
18 months if you apply on or after 1 January 2027
It will allow you to stay for 3 years if you completed a PhD or other doctoral qualification.

CRITICAL — Graduate Visa Rules:
- The Graduate visa CANNOT be extended under any circumstances. It is a one-time visa. Once it expires you must leave the UK or switch to a different visa category (such as the Skilled Worker visa).
- Time spent on the Graduate visa does NOT count towards the 5 years of continuous residence required for Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR / UK settlement). Only time on eligible long-term visas like the Skilled Worker visa counts toward settlement.
- After Your Graduate Visa — The Skilled Worker Route: If you secure a job offer from a UK Home Office-licensed employer, you can apply for a Skilled Worker visa. As of April 2024, the minimum salary threshold for a Skilled Worker visa is £38,700 per year (or the 'going rate' for the specific job, whichever is higher). This is a major increase from the previous £26,200 threshold and has significantly affected international graduates' ability to stay.
- Graduate Visa Work Conditions: On the Graduate visa, you have much greater freedom than on the Student visa. You can work in ANY job at ANY skill level (including unskilled work), switch employers freely at any time without any restrictions, and you are also permitted to be self-employed or start and run your own business. You cannot claim public funds.

When you can travel to the UK
You can arrive before your course starts. This can be either:

up to 1 week before, if your course lasts 6 months or less
up to 1 month before, if your course lasts more than 6 months
You must not travel to the UK before the start date given on your visa, no matter when your course starts.

Fees
It costs:

£558 to apply for a Student visa from outside the UK
£558 to extend or switch to a Student visa from inside the UK
You must pay the visa fee for each person that joins you.

Healthcare surcharge
You’ll also have to pay the healthcare surcharge as part of your application.

How much you pay depends on how long your visa lasts. For students, this cost is strictly £776 per year of your visa. For example, a 3-year undergraduate degree will require an upfront IHS payment of £2,328.

NHS Access: Paying the IHS grants you full access to the National Health Service (NHS) on the same basis as a UK resident. This means GP appointments, hospital treatment, A&E visits, and prescriptions are all covered at no additional charge for the duration of your visa. This is one of the most significant financial benefits of the UK Student visa.

Registering with a GP: Access to the NHS is NOT automatic. You must actively register with a local General Practitioner (GP / family doctor) shortly after arriving in the UK. Without registering, you cannot book appointments or access NHS services even though you have paid the IHS. To register, find a GP surgery near your home or university using the NHS website (nhs.uk), and bring your passport and proof of UK address. Registration is free.

Your partner and children
As of January 2024, international students on a standard taught course (like a Bachelor's or taught Master's) are NOT allowed to bring dependants (partners or children) to the UK. You can only bring your partner and children (‘dependants’) if you are studying a PhD, a doctoral qualification, or a research-based higher degree.

What you can and cannot do
You can:

study
work as a student union sabbatical officer
You may be able to work - how much depends on what you’re studying and whether you’re working in or out of term-time. The amount you can work is strictly capped based on your study level:
- Degree level or above: Maximum of 20 hours per week during term time.
- Below degree level: Maximum of 10 hours per week during term time.
You are permitted to work full-time during official university holidays.

Proving Your Right to Work — The Share Code:
Because the UK has fully moved to an eVisa system, you can no longer prove your right to work simply by showing your passport or a physical visa sticker. Instead, you must log into your UKVI online account and generate a 'share code.' You give this share code to your employer, who enters it along with your date of birth on the UK Government's online checking service to verify your work permission. Students who are unaware of this process are often turned away on their first day of work. You can generate a new share code at any time from your UKVI account.

You cannot:

claim public funds (benefits) and pensions
work in certain jobs, for example as a professional sportsperson or sports coach
be self-employed
study at an academy or a local authority-funded school (also known as a maintained school)
If your application is successful, you’ll be told what you can and cannot do on a Student visa.

Council Tax Exemption:
Full-time students in the UK are completely exempt from paying Council Tax, which is a significant local government tax that can cost hundreds of pounds per year. However, this exemption is NOT automatic. You must obtain a Council Tax exemption certificate from your university and submit it to your local council. If you live with non-students, the exemption rules become more complex and they may still be liable.





Your course
You must have an unconditional offer of a place on a course with a licensed student sponsor.

To prove this, your education provider will send you a reference number (called a Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS)) once they’ve offered you a place on the course. You need a CAS before you can apply for your visa.

Courses you can study
You can do one of the following courses:

a full-time course leading to a qualification that’s below degree level (RQF level 3, 4 or 5) with at least 15 hours a week of organised daytime study

a full-time course leading to a qualification that’s degree level or above (RQF level 6, 7 or 8)

a full-time course at degree level or above (RQF level 6,7 or 8) that’s equivalent to a UK higher education course and is being delivered as part of a longer course overseas

a part-time course leading to a qualification that’s above degree level (RQF level 7 or above)

an English language course at level B2 or above in the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages

You can also apply for this visa if you’re taking up a full-time elected position as a Student Union Sabbatical Officer.

You may also need an Academic Technology Approval Scheme (ATAS) certificate if you’re studying or researching sensitive topics at RQF level 7 or above.

The qualification levels are different in Scotland.

Your Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS)
Once they’ve offered you a place on the course, your education provider will send you a reference number called a Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies.

You must enter this reference number on your visa application.

You must apply for your visa within 6 months of receiving your CAS.

Sponsor Licence Revocation — What Happens If Your University Loses Its Licence:
If your university or college has its student sponsor licence revoked by the UK Home Office, your Student visa will be curtailed (cut short). You will typically be given 60 days to either find a new licensed student sponsor and transfer to a new course, or leave the UK. The Home Office will notify you by email, which is another reason to always keep your contact details updated with your institution and UKVI.

Curtailment After Withdrawing From Your Course:
If you drop out or withdraw from your course for any reason, your university is legally required to report this to the Home Office within 10 days. The Home Office will then curtail (cut short) your Student visa. You will typically be given 60 days from the date of the curtailment notice to either enrol in a new course with a licensed sponsor and apply for a new visa, or leave the UK. You cannot quietly remain in the UK on your existing visa after withdrawing from your course.

Student Finance England Ineligibility:
International students on a UK Student visa are completely ineligible for UK government student loans from Student Finance England. This means no Tuition Fee Loans, Maintenance Loans, or any other government-funded student finance. International students must fund their entire education through personal or family funds, institutional scholarships awarded directly by the university, country-specific government scholarships (e.g. Chevening, Commonwealth), or private international student loans (which typically require proof of enrollment and may have high interest rates).

National Insurance Number (NIN):
To work legally in the UK and have your taxes and National Insurance contributions correctly recorded, you need a National Insurance Number (NIN) — the UK equivalent of the US Social Security Number. Here is what students need to know:
- You can START work before receiving your NIN, as long as you have your right-to-work share code and your employer has verified it.
- You must apply for a NIN once you have a job. You can apply online through the UK Government website (gov.uk) and will be asked to complete an online application and potentially a phone interview.
- Your employer will need your NIN to correctly process your pay and tax deductions. Without it, you may be put on an emergency tax code and overpay tax initially (which can be reclaimed).
- Your NIN stays with you for life. If you return to the UK in the future, you use the same number.

UK Income Tax for Student Workers:
International students working in the UK are subject to UK income tax on their UK earnings. Key figures for the 2024/25 tax year:
- Personal Allowance: The first £12,570 you earn in a tax year is completely tax-free.
- Basic Rate: Earnings between £12,571 and £50,270 are taxed at 20%.
- The UK tax year runs from 6 April to 5 April the following year.
- Emergency Tax Code: If you start a new job without an NIN, your employer may put you on an emergency tax code (such as 1257L W1/M1 or BR), which can result in you paying too much tax. Once you provide your NIN, your tax code should be corrected. Any overpaid tax can be reclaimed from HMRC (His Majesty's Revenue and Customs) either through your employer or directly via a self-assessment tax return.
- Students working part-time are unlikely to exceed the £12,570 personal allowance and may pay little or no tax, but should still ensure their tax code is correct.
- Scotland has its own slightly different income tax rates and bands, which apply if you live in Scotland.

Opening a UK Bank Account:
A UK bank account is essential for receiving wages, paying rent, and managing daily finances. Most major banks offer dedicated international student accounts, but the process is different from simply walking in:
- What you typically need: Your passport, your eVisa share code or confirmation of immigration status, your university CAS letter or official enrollment confirmation, and proof of UK address (this can be a university letter or tenancy agreement).
- Recommended banks for international students: Barclays, HSBC, NatWest, Santander, and Lloyds all offer international student accounts. Many universities have partnership deals with specific banks that speed up the process.
- Digital banks: Apps like Monzo, Starling, and Revolut are often the easiest option for students to set up quickly on arrival, as they have fewer document requirements. However, some landlords and employers prefer a traditional bank account.
- You generally cannot open a UK bank account before arriving in the UK, though some banks allow a pre-arrival application to be started online.
- Without a UK bank account, you may also struggle to apply for a NIN, as proof of a UK bank account is sometimes requested.

Driving in the UK:
- Foreign Driving Licence: International students can legally drive in the UK on their valid home country driving licence for up to 12 months from the date they arrive in the UK.
- After 12 Months: If you remain in the UK after 12 months, you must get a UK driving licence to continue driving legally. The process depends on your home country:
  - EU/EEA licence holders: Can exchange their licence for a UK licence without taking a test.
  - Non-EU/EEA licence holders (e.g. India, Pakistan, USA, Australia): Cannot simply exchange their licence. They must apply for a provisional UK licence, pass the UK theory test, and pass the UK practical driving test.
- International Driving Permit (IDP): An IDP can supplement your foreign licence but does not replace the 12-month rule or allow you to drive beyond 12 months without a UK licence.
- Insurance: You must have valid UK car insurance to drive. Inform your insurer you are an international licence holder, as this affects your premium.









Money you need
You must have enough money to pay for your course and support yourself in the UK.

How much money you need depends on your circumstances and what you’re applying for.

Course fee
You need enough money to pay for your course for 1 academic year (up to 9 months). The amount you need to pay will be on your Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS).

If you’ve been in the UK with a valid visa for at least 12 months, you do not need to prove you have this money for your visa application.

Money to support yourself (‘financial requirement’)
You’ll need to show you have enough money to support yourself - unless you’ve been in the UK with a valid visa for at least 12 months on the date of your application.

How much money you need depends on where you will be studying. You’ll need either:

£1,529 per month (for up to 9 months) for courses in London
£1,171 per month (for up to 9 months) for courses outside London
If you’re boarding at a residential independent school, you’ll need to pay boarding fees instead. The amount you need to pay will be on your CAS.

London means the City of London and the 32 London boroughs.

You'll need to prove you have extra money for each family member you bring with you. The exact additional amounts required per dependant are:
- £845 per month (for up to 9 months) if the dependant will be living in London.
- £680 per month (for up to 9 months) if the dependant will be living outside London.
For example, a PhD student in London bringing one spouse and one child would need to prove an additional £845 x 9 x 2 = £15,210 on top of their own maintenance funds. These funds must meet the same 28-day bank statement rule as the student's own maintenance funds.

You must have this money for at least 28 days in a row. The end date of the 28-day period must be within 31 days of the date you apply for your visa.

Example

If you submit your application on 1 January, you’d have to show that the funds were in your bank account for at least the 28-day period ending on 1 December.

If you have a student loan or financial sponsorship, you’ll need to provide evidence of this from your loan or sponsorship company.

Read the guidance on finances for student applications for more information about the money you need and how to prove it.

How to prove your knowledge of English
You can prove it in one of the following ways:

having a UK school qualification
having a degree from a UK institution
having a degree from an institution that was outside the UK and taught in English
passing an English test from an approved provider
If you have a UK school qualification
You can prove your knowledge of English if you have a qualification from a UK school that you began when you were under 18. You must have one of the following qualifications:

a GCSE
an A level
Scottish National Qualification level 4 or 5
Scottish Higher or Advanced Higher in English
If you have a degree from a UK institution
You can prove your knowledge of English if you have a degree-level qualification that was both:

taught in English
awarded by a UK institution, even if you studied outside the UK
If you have a degree from an institution outside the UK
If your degree was taught in English and was not awarded by a UK institution, you’ll need to apply for an assessment from Ecctis.

Ecctis will give you a code to confirm that your qualification is equivalent to a UK bachelor’s degree or higher and was taught in English.

Passing an English test
If you do not have any qualifications that prove your knowledge of English, you will need to pass a Secure English Language Test (SELT) from an approved provider, such as "IELTS for UKVI". Standard IELTS is not always accepted.

English Test Validity — 2-Year Expiry Rule:
All SELT results (including IELTS for UKVI) are only valid for 2 years from the date of the test. If your test result is older than 2 years at the time you submit your Student visa application, UKVI will reject it as invalid and your application may be refused. This is a very common trap for students who took their English test early and then deferred their university admission by a year or more. Always check your test date before applying and retake the test if necessary.

You must prove you can read, write, speak and understand English to a certain level on the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) scale. 

What you’re studying	Level
Degree level or above	Equivalent to CEFR level B2 (Minimum of IELTS 5.5 in all four components: Reading, Writing, Listening, Speaking)
Below degree level	CEFR level B1 (Minimum of IELTS 4.0 in all four components)
If you’re studying with a Higher Education Provider
If you’re studying at degree level or above, your Higher Education Provider (HEP) can assess your level of English themselves. This means they may ask you to do a different test. 

This must still be equivalent to a CEFR level B2. 



Documents you'll need to apply
When you apply for your Student visa you must provide:

a current passport or other valid travel documentation
a Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS) from your course provider
You may also need to provide:

proof you have enough money to support yourself and pay for your course - this will vary depending on your circumstances
a valid ATAS certificate if your course and nationality require it. ATAS (Academic Technology Approval Scheme) is a UK government security clearance required for certain postgraduate students (RQF level 7 and above) studying in sensitive subject areas. It applies regardless of whether you are studying for a taught or research postgraduate degree. Subjects that typically require an ATAS certificate include: advanced materials science and engineering, aerospace and defence technology, military and dual-use technologies, nuclear sciences and engineering, quantum computing and communications, artificial intelligence and robotics (certain advanced research areas), and satellite and space technology. ATAS certificates are free to apply for and are obtained via the UK government website before submitting your visa application. Most Western countries (EU/EEA, USA, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, Singapore) are exempt from the ATAS requirement. If ATAS is required and you do not have a valid certificate, your visa application will be automatically refused.
proof of parental or other legal guardian consent if you’re under 18
proof of your relationship to your parent or guardian if you’re under 18
your tuberculosis test results — IMPORTANT: Students from certain countries MUST provide a TB (Tuberculosis) test certificate from a UK Visas and Immigration-approved clinic as part of their visa application. This is a non-negotiable requirement for residents of countries where TB is prevalent. The test must be booked via an official list of clinics published on the UK Government website. The certificate must be valid at the time of your application and must show that you are free from active TB. A standard doctor's letter, diagnostic imaging reports, or tests done outside of an approved clinic are strictly rejected by UKVI and will result in an automatic refusal. Check the specific list of countries on the UK Gov website to see if you are required to submit this evidence.
written consent for your application from your financial sponsor if you’ve received sponsorship for your course fees and living costs in the last 12 months
You may need to provide additional documents depending on your circumstances. Read the guidance for the full list of documents you’ll need to provide.

Extend your visa
You may be able to extend your Student visa to stay longer and continue your course or study a new course. This includes if you currently have a Tier 4 (General) student visa.

To extend your visa you must:

be in the UK on a Student visa or a Tier 4 (General) student visa
have an unconditional offer of a place on a course with a licensed student sponsor - shown by your Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS)
show that your studies are at a higher academic level than your current course (called the ‘academic progress requirement’) - there are some exceptions
If you’ve been in the UK with a valid visa for less than 12 months, you’ll need to prove you have enough money to support yourself in the UK.

Section 3C Leave — Continuing to Study and Work During a Pending Application:
If you apply to extend your Student visa before your current visa expires, you are automatically granted 'Section 3C leave.' This means your existing visa is automatically extended on the same conditions while UKVI processes your application. You can legally continue to study and work under the same rules (e.g., 20 hours/week if degree level) during this entire waiting period. CRITICAL: Section 3C leave only applies if you submitted your application BEFORE your current visa expired. If your visa expires before you apply, you have no legal right to remain.

Your partner or children will need to apply separately to extend their visa. They can either apply at the same time as you, or at any time before their current visa expires.

You can also switch to a Graduate visa. This will allow you  to stay in the UK after successfully completing your course for:

2 years if you apply on or before 31 December 2026
18 months if you apply on or after 1 January 2027
It will allow you to stay for 3 years if you completed a PhD or other doctoral qualification.

Showing academic progress
If you’re currently studying in the UK, you’ll usually need to show your studies will be at a higher academic level than your current course.

Your new course must be one of the following:

at a higher academic level than your current course
at the same level and related to your previous course or career aspirations - it must be degree level or above at a Higher Education Provider (HEP)
intercalated to a medicine, dentistry or medical science course you started studying under your Student visa (including a Tier 4 (General) student visa)
You do not need to show your studies are at a higher level if you’re doing one of the following:

resitting exams or repeating modules
applying for the first time to a new institution to complete a course you started at an institution that lost its student sponsorship licence
applying after working as a student union sabbatical officer to complete a qualification you started studying under your last Student visa (including a Tier 4 (General) student visa)
completing a PhD or other doctorate that you started studying under your last Student visa (including a Tier 4 (General) student visa)
continuing your medical, dentistry or medical science degree after completing an intercalated course
applying to extend your stay to complete your studies because you’ve done (or want to do) a work placement or study abroad programme
Read the guidance for more information about when you need to prove your studies are at a higher level.

Eligibility
To switch to a Student visa you must:

be in the UK
have an unconditional offer of a place on a course with a licensed student sponsor - shown by your Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS)
If you’ve been in the UK with a valid visa for less than 12 months, you’ll need to prove you have enough money to support yourself in the UK.

Your partner or children will need to apply separately to switch their visa. They can either apply at the same time as you, or at any time before their current visa expires.

When to apply
The earliest you can apply is 3 months before your course starts.

You must apply before your current visa expires. Your new course must begin within 28 days of your current visa expiring.

For example, if your visa expires on 1 December, you must apply for a new visa before 1 December. Your new course must begin by 29 December.

You must also apply within 6 months of getting a Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS).

You can stay in the UK until you get your decision.

If you applied for an administrative review because your application was refused, you can stay in the UK until you get your review decision.

Fees
For each person, you’ll need to pay:

£558 to switch to this visa
the healthcare surcharge
You’ll need to have your fingerprints and a photo (biometric information) taken - there’s no fee for this.

Apply
You must apply online.

As part of your application you’ll need to prove your identity.

How you do this depends on where you’re from and the type of passport you have.

You’ll either:

use the ‘UK Immigration: ID Check’ app to scan your identity document - you’ll also create or sign in to your UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) account
have your fingerprints and photo (biometric information) taken at a UK Visa and Citizenship Application Services (UKVCAS) service point
You’ll be told what you need to do when you apply.

Start now

You must not travel outside of the UK, Ireland, the Channel Islands or the Isle of Man until you get a decision. Your application will be withdrawn if you do.

How long it takes to get a decision
A decision will usually be made within 8 weeks.

You may be able to pay to get a faster decision.

After you apply
You’ll be contacted if your application is complex and will take longer, for example because:

your supporting documents need to be verified
you need to attend an interview
of your personal circumstances (for example if you have a criminal conviction)
If you need to change something in your application after you’ve sent it contact UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI).

You can ask to withdraw your application by contacting UKVI. Your fee will only be refunded if UKVI has not started processing your application.

If your application is successful
You’ll get an eVisa (a digital record of your identity and immigration status).

Your decision email or letter will tell you how to get access to your eVisa. You’ll need to create a UKVI account.

Police Registration Scheme Abolished (August 2022 Update):
Historically, students from countries such as India, Pakistan, China, Turkey, and several other Middle Eastern/South Asian nations were required to register with the local police within 7 days of their arrival in the UK.
- Important: This police registration requirement was completely abolished in August 2022.
- No action is needed: International students of all nationalities are no longer required to register with the police. If you see old visa templates or guidebooks mentioning police registration, you can safely ignore them. You do not need to attend a police station, pay any registration fee, or notify them of your presence. Your information is now tracked purely digitally via the eVisa system.
